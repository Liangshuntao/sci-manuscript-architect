# Innovation Taxonomy

## Purpose

Use this reference to classify what kind of innovation the biomedical paper can
credibly claim and what evidence threshold each type requires.

## Innovation Types

- **Conceptual innovation**: reframes a disease process, biological state,
  cell population, or pathophysiological model.
- **Mechanistic innovation**: provides causal or strongly supported mechanistic
  evidence for genes, pathways, cells, interactions, or phenotypes.
- **Methodological innovation**: introduces a new experimental, computational,
  statistical, or analytical approach.
- **Translational innovation**: connects findings to diagnosis, prognosis,
  therapy, drug target, patient stratification, or clinical decision-making.
- **Resource innovation**: provides a reusable atlas, cohort, dataset,
  benchmark, tool, or workflow.
- **Integrative innovation**: combines modalities or evidence layers to answer a
  question that single-layer analysis could not answer.

## Minimum Evidence Expectations

- Conceptual: clear contrast with prior models and broad evidence alignment.
- Mechanistic: perturbation, validation, causal design, temporal evidence, or
  convergent multi-layer support.
- Methodological: reproducible method, benchmark, comparison, and failure-mode
  analysis.
- Translational: clinical cohort, outcome association, independent validation,
  or guideline/target relevance.
- Resource: data quality, coverage, reproducibility, documentation, and reuse
  potential.
- Integrative: explicit reason each data layer changes the conclusion.

## Output

Report:

- Primary and secondary innovation type.
- Evidence currently supporting each innovation claim.
- Evidence missing for higher-tier framing.
- Claims that should be downgraded or removed.
- Recommended wording strength: exploratory, associative, mechanistic,
  predictive, translational, or clinical.
