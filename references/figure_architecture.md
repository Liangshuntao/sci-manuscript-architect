# Figure Architecture

## Purpose

Use this reference to decide what the manuscript's figures must accomplish
before plotting or drafting figure captions.

## Core Figure Types

- **Study design figure**: cohort, samples, experimental flow, timepoints,
  omics assays, validation sets.
- **Workflow figure**: computational pipeline, quality control, integration,
  statistical modeling, validation path.
- **Mechanistic model**: cells, pathways, ligand-receptor interactions,
  regulatory circuits, metabolic states, spatial niches, or causal model.
- **Main finding figure**: the strongest result linked to the central claim.
- **Validation figure**: independent cohort, orthogonal assay, perturbation,
  model system, or clinical association.
- **Graphical abstract**: simplified final narrative for journal-facing use.

## Design Rules

- Every main claim needs a figure, table, or explicit evidence pointer.
- Figure order should match the manuscript logic, not analysis chronology.
- Avoid introducing a new story in a figure that is absent from the
  Introduction or Results.
- Distinguish discovery from validation visually.
- Keep colors, group names, and abbreviations consistent across figures.
- Captions must explain biological meaning, not only plotting methods.

## Output

Produce a Figure Architecture Plan with:

- Figure number and type.
- Claim supported by the figure.
- Required panels.
- Data source and statistical evidence.
- Caption function.
- Risks: missing validation, overclaim, crowded figure, unclear comparator.
