# Biomedical Introduction Flowchart

## Purpose

Use this reference to design the Introduction before writing prose. Adapted
from Supervisor-Skills' Introduction flowchart, but translated into biomedical
SCI logic.

## Six-Part Flow

1. **Clinical or biological context**: define disease, biological process,
   clinical scenario, cell state, pathway, or assay context.
2. **Unmet need**: explain what remains unresolved for patients, biology,
   diagnosis, prognosis, therapy, model systems, or analysis methods.
3. **Knowledge gap**: identify the precise missing answer in prior work.
4. **Study objective or hypothesis**: state what this study tests, maps,
   explains, predicts, or validates.
5. **Study design and key approach**: summarize cohort, model, omics layer,
   experimental design, computational framework, or validation strategy.
6. **Contribution and claim boundary**: state the main contribution and what
   the study does not claim.

## Integrity Checks

- The unmet need must be supported by field scan, guidelines, or prior studies.
- The knowledge gap must not be a generic "few studies" statement.
- The objective must be answerable by the user's actual materials.
- The contribution must map to evidence in the Evidence Ledger.
- Clinical or causal wording must be downgraded if validation is insufficient.

## Output

Produce a paragraph-level outline with:

- Function of each paragraph.
- Key claims and evidence source.
- Required citations or `VERIFY`.
- Claim-strength label.
- Risks: `FIELD_SCAN_REQUIRED`, `OVERCLAIM`, `EVIDENCE_INCOMPLETE`.
