# Submission Severity Audit

## Purpose

Use this reference near the end of drafting or when the user asks whether the
manuscript is ready to submit. It converts reviewer concerns into
`CRITICAL`, `MAJOR`, and `MINOR` findings.

## Severity Levels

- **CRITICAL**: should block submission. Examples: unsupported central claim,
  missing sample size or ethics information, invalid statistical design,
  fabricated or unverified citation, no evidence for clinical/causal wording,
  missing required reporting-guideline item.
- **MAJOR**: likely reviewer concern but potentially fixable before submission.
  Examples: weak gap framing, incomplete validation, unclear figure logic,
  missing key comparator, Discussion overreach, inconsistent terminology.
- **MINOR**: polish issue. Examples: awkward wording, caption detail, formatting,
  abbreviation inconsistency, minor grammar issue.

## Audit Dimensions

- Field position and motivation.
- Claim-to-evidence mapping.
- Study design and statistics.
- Reporting guideline completeness.
- Figure architecture and captions.
- Citation support and reference accuracy.
- Language and journal style.
- LaTeX, labels, tables, and supplementary materials.

## Output

Produce:

- Severity-ranked findings.
- Exact location or artifact affected.
- Why it matters to reviewers.
- Fix recommendation.
- Whether it is feasible in 72 hours.
- Submission recommendation: submit, submit after fixes, major revision needed,
  or do not submit yet.
